# Molded Pulp for Oji India: Segment Prioritisation and Pilot Proposal

**Independent Business Research | Jayanth Ram | IIT Hyderabad | October 2026**

An independent desk-research project assessing India's molded pulp packaging market, comparing potential customer segments, and proposing a practical market-entry pilot.

## Research Question

Which of three Indian packaging segments should be prioritised for a molded-pulp pilot: electronics and e-commerce protective packaging, food and FMCG packaging, or automotive and industrial components? What must a six-month pilot prove before a wider commercial launch is considered?

## Executive Summary

**Preliminary recommendation: prioritise food and FMCG packaging, narrowly, while retaining electronics and e-commerce protective packaging as the alternative.**

The weighted segment-scoring model gives food and FMCG a base score of **3.30/5**, compared with **3.20/5** for electronics and **2.35/5** for automotive and industrial components.

The lead is small and reverses when commercial feasibility and adoption are weighted more heavily. The recommendation is therefore provisional rather than definitive.

The proposed pilot tests whether Indian buyers will accept a price premium for fibre-based packaging that meets their barrier, leak-proof and functional requirements.

## Project Scope

The research evaluates three potential customer segments:

- **Electronics and e-commerce:** Protective trays, inserts and cushioning applications.
- **Food and FMCG:** Fibre-based trays and packaging applications requiring relevant barrier or leak-proof performance.
- **Automotive and industrial components:** Protective trays and inserts for components and equipment.

The analysis combines public-source research, competitor benchmarking, weighted segment scoring, market-sizing scenarios and a proposed six-month pilot.

## Methodology

1. Review company product information, government publications, industry-association data and commercial market research.
2. Record sources, extracted claims, reliability assessments and limitations.
3. Benchmark eight competitors or alternative packaging solutions.
4. Compare three customer segments using six weighted criteria.
5. Build low, base and high market-sizing scenarios using sourced figures and explicitly labelled assumptions.
6. Conduct sensitivity analysis to understand whether the segment recommendation changes under different weights.
7. Propose a six-month pilot with customer-validation activities, success metrics, risks and decision criteria.

## Preliminary Findings

- Food and FMCG leads under the base and demand-heavy weighting scenarios.
- Electronics and e-commerce leads when feasibility receives greater weight.
- Automotive and industrial components score lower under all three weighting scenarios.
- Existing suppliers and substitute materials create competitive pressure across the segments.
- Public market-size estimates are inconsistent, limiting confidence in precise market-sizing conclusions.

The most important unresolved question is whether prospective buyers will accept the price of a suitable fibre-based solution relative to their incumbent packaging.

## Proposed Six-Month Pilot

The proposed pilot focuses on four hypotheses:

1. **Customer willingness to pay:** Conduct 8–10 buyer interviews to investigate acceptable price premiums.
2. **Technical performance:** Test relevant samples against buyer-specified leak, grease and moisture requirements.
3. **Commercial feasibility:** Compare indicative landed costs across import, local production and incumbent alternatives.
4. **Buyer commitment:** Seek a letter of intent or paid trial from at least one prospective customer.

The proposed budget is **₹8.25 lakh**, including a 10% contingency. This is an initial planning assumption, not a verified supplier quotation or approved company budget.

The pilot thresholds and budget require validation before implementation.

## Limitations and Evidence Quality

This project is based on independent desk research. It does not include primary customer interviews, verified supplier quotations, laboratory testing or confirmed purchase commitments.

The commercial market-research sources used for segment sizing conflict with one another. The resulting estimates are treated cautiously rather than presented as a single verified market value.

Some market-sizing inputs are analyst assumptions because reliable public data was unavailable. Competitor information also varies in reliability, and company descriptions do not independently establish product performance or commercial capability.

The findings are preliminary hypotheses for further validation, not a confirmed market-entry decision.
